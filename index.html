01 · About

Building a research identity around the physics of light.

My research began with transparent electrodes for photovoltaic devices, but the questions that kept drawing me in were optical: why a few nanometres can change an entire spectrum, how interfaces redistribute electromagnetic fields, and where ideal simulations diverge from fabricated structures.

During my M.Sc. at IIT Bombay, I deliberately shifted my thesis toward optical design and light–matter interaction. I now want to develop as an experimental physicist who can move confidently between numerical models, fabrication, spectroscopy and complex instrumentation.

I value careful physical interpretation over exaggerated results. In my work, simulated gains are treated as design limits, while morphology, roughness, interface quality, process variation and measured optical constants determine what is experimentally achievable.

M.Sc. Thesis · NCPRE, IIT Bombay

Simulation and Optical Design of Multilayer Transparent Conducting Oxides

I investigated IZO/Ag/IZO oxide–metal–oxide electrodes for photovoltaic devices, combining wavelength-dependent optical modelling with fabrication-aware analysis. The work examined interference, parasitic absorption, ultrathin-metal optical behaviour, transmitted photon flux and the conductivity–transparency trade-off.
